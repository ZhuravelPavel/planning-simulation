# How marketing and monetization works

Marketing is how a property is presented and how people are brought to it. Monetization is how that visit, or that use, becomes money. Both apply to every site and every project. They are not a plan for one rebuild.

Advertising is one way a property makes money. How an impression is served and recorded is in the advertising guide. Search is one way a property gets its audience. How pages and queries are organized is in the search guide. This chapter is the commercial pattern those two sit inside.

There is no separate brand department and no company mailing list. A property speaks on itself.

Pavel is the Ads, Marketing, and SEO lead, for every property. Rotchilda is the project manager when a commercial change crosses sites, needs development, or has to reach the owner.

## The ways a property makes money

A property uses one of these, or both. It does not have to use both.

**Advertising.** The company is the publisher. An advertiser pays to reach people who are already on the site. The same ad server can carry more than one property. Each property is its own publisher, with its own sheet. A contract is either a price per thousand impressions or a share of what the advertiser later earns. The sheet is the commercial record. The company’s books, which Melissa keeps, are the cash. Those are different numbers, on every property, for the same reason: one is delivery valued at the deal, the other is what arrived.

**A direct payment.** A person pays the company for something the product does. That can be a membership, a tool, or a shop order. The payment is not a fee to remove ads. If a property shows no ads to someone who is logged in, that is a rule about the page. The thing being sold is the product. Hiding ads only for people who pay would turn the payment into an advertising product.

A direct payment is recorded as the company’s money. The card statement says Forward Handle LLC. Where the product can be described without naming an adult site, the receipt follows that description. Pavel recommends the shape. Sakura decides it.

A shop is a checkout. It is not an advertiser and it does not go into another property’s advertising book.

## How marketing works on any site

Marketing decides what the property is for, who it is for, and what a visitor can do without paying. Acquisition follows from that.

On a property whose audience comes from Google, marketing and search are the same effort. Pavel owns both. The search guide is how that property’s pages are organized. On a property whose audience arrives directly, or from the community, search is not the engine. The property is still marketed: by what the page itself says, and by what regular users tell each other.

When a commercial change is visible, it is explained on that property. The channels are the ones the site already has: its blog, a banner, an update on the page. There is no list to mail. The explanation is about the product, not about the servers or the billing machinery. Something still being built is described as coming. Something that is live is described as present. A date is not announced until it is real.

Pavel writes that explanation for whichever site is changing. Tickets that follow are Ezra’s, along with every other user ticket. If a ticket is a bug, he and Simon work it together.

## How monetization is kept consistent

Pavel decides, per property, which of the two methods apply and what the payment is for. He does not invent a new commercial model for each project. A new site inherits the pattern: sell the audience, sell a product, or both, and do not sell the absence of ads.

Where a property does both, the audiences stay distinct. Advertising is priced against people who are using the free property. A payment is priced against people who want more of the product. Simon implements the distinction on the page, on any site, because he is the development lead. Marko carries it out inside Revive only where the change is a creative or a zone link that already exists. That split is in the advertising guide.

The price, the processor, and a partner that carries a large share of advertising are the decisions that leave Pavel’s desk. They go to Sakura in the same form every time: what is happening, why it matters, the real options, and the recommendation. Rotchilda assembles that. Adam is included when the decision describes the company to a bank, a processor, or a contract.
